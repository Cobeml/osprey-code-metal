# Forge 2026 Saturday mission brief

Source: organizer briefing, November 7, 2026, BASE-X, Boston. The original 24-slide PDF is stored beside the local checkout because it contains venue access details.

## Operation Lost and Found (slides 3–5)

Search and rescue in dangerous environments without reliable GPS, cellular service, or fixed communications. The organizers invite teams to tackle one objective or a focused part of it.

1. Locate isolated people.
2. Detect signs of life and hazards.
3. Map changing conditions.
4. Deliver critical information over degraded or overloaded channels.

## Build window and submission (slides 7–9, 18, 22)

All times are Boston local time (EST).

- **Saturday, November 7 at 10:00 AM:** Build starts. Teams can have up to four people.
- **Sunday, November 8 at 11:00 AM:** Final submission instructions.
- **Sunday at noon:** Hard submission cutoff. The organizer-provisioned team repository is the submission. All code must be pushed there by the cutoff. A live demo is recommended and a backup video is optional.
- **Sunday, noon–2:00 PM:** Table judging, about 15 minutes per team. Keep a teammate at the table.
- **Sunday at 2:45 PM:** Five finalists present for 10 minutes each. Awards are scheduled around 4:15 PM.

Existing projects, open source, and AI coding tools are allowed. Judges score work done during the 26-hour build window. Be explicit about what already existed and use commit history to show what was built at the event. The project checkout linked in this index and the organizer-provisioned submission repository serve separate purposes.

## Judging (slides 11–13)

Four equally weighted criteria, each scored from 1 to 5 in both rounds, for 20 points total.

| Criterion | What to show |
| --- | --- |
| Real-world relevance | A specific user, problem, operating environment, and useful capability |
| Working live demo | Functionality built during the event. Partial progress on a hard problem can score. |
| Deployability | A credible path to field use, including hardware, power, latency, bandwidth, and reliability |
| Ideation | A fresh approach, novel combination, or clever use of available hardware |

At the table, show what works, distinguish prior work from weekend work, explain gaps, and describe what field deployment would take.

## Build and hardware guidance (slides 15–17, 21–23)

The organizer plan calls for choosing a problem by Saturday noon, testing risky components by 6:00 PM, integrating by Sunday 2:00 AM, and using Sunday morning to stabilize and rehearse the demo. Keep a replay or simulated fallback for unavailable hardware.

Kits are signed out to teams, and all checked-out items must be returned before leaving Sunday. The brief lists a Raspberry Pi 5 with active cooling and AI HAT, limited Jetson Nanos, and shared compute, radio, imaging, sensing, and test equipment. Drones fly only in daylight with Code Metal staff. Discord carries organizer updates and schedule changes. Hardware model numbers and actual team checkout remain to be confirmed.
